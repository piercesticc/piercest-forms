Administrative — cover up front

Walk through these before opening the intake conversation.

Limits to Confidentiality

Read aloud / paraphrase: Everything here is confidential, with a few exceptions. Confidentiality may be broken if:
  • Confidentiality may be broken if…
    • 1) You authorize the release of information
    • 2) Your therapist is ordered by a court to release information
    • 3) You present a physical danger to yourself or others
    • 4) Abuse of a child, dependent adult, or elder is suspected
    • 5) The therapist learns of the downloading or accessing of child pornography
    • 6) You have a medical emergency
    • 7) You are trying to get insurance reimbursement

Policies & Logistics

  • Cancellation policy
    • Appointments are weekly and 50 minutes.
    • Inform me of a cancellation at least 24 hours in advance; otherwise you will be charged for the session.
  • Recording sessions
    • For training purposes, on an external audio device; gets erased; confidential.
    • "Your meetings may be audio or video recorded for the purpose of supervision and/or training."
    • "I want to be clear about what the recordings are for: my supervisor is evaluating me — how I'm showing up and the way I'm being with you in session. They're not analyzing you or what you share. The focus is on my work as a therapist, not on your content."
  • Timeline
    • I will be at Pierce through May 2027.
    • After that, you'd have the option to come with me wherever I go next — but that's something we'd figure out together when the time comes.
  • Billing
    • Appears as VTG*CIIS on your statement.
    • Charged same or next day.
    • Contact me if you have any issues.
  • Communication
    • Text or call me on my confidential line. Email is fine too.
  • Telehealth
    • Confirm the location where the client is joining (noted in header above).

Set the frame & open the intake

"We have 50 minutes and can start wherever you'd like — and we have an intake form we can start with if you prefer."
"There's no wrong place to begin. Some of what I'll ask is just the standard intake stuff, and some of it we'll let unfold naturally as we talk. If anything I ask feels like too much, you can always tell me to slow down or skip it."
Easing in: "Before we get into any of the forms — what made you decide to reach out for therapy right now?"
3

Intake Form

Fill in as the conversation unfolds — you don't need to go top to bottom.

Contact Information

Presenting Concerns

"What brings you in?" · "What's been going on that made now feel like the right time?" · "If we could focus on one thing together, what would feel most important to you?"
Following up gently: "Tell me more about that." · "What does that look like day to day for you?" · "How long has this been weighing on you?"
"If our work together went really well, what would be different in your life?" · "What are you hoping therapy can help you with?" · "When you imagine feeling better, what does that look like?"
"How are you feeling about being here today?" · "A lot of people feel some nerves or mixed feelings about starting — is any of that here for you?" · "Is there anything you're worried about when it comes to therapy or working with me?"

Therapy History

Previous or concurrent therapy — when, length, issues, what the experience was like, what was most/least helpful, reasons for termination.
"Have you been in therapy before?" · If yes: "What was that experience like for you?" · "What worked well, and what didn't?" · "What would you want to be different this time?"

Psychiatric & Psychological History

Easing in: "I'm going to ask a few more clinical questions now — these are routine and I ask everyone. Just answer what feels comfortable."
"Have you ever been hospitalized for mental health reasons, or worked with a psychiatrist?" · "Has there ever been a time things felt really out of control or hard to manage?"
Safety: If any current suicidal feelings, make sure client has the Suicide Prevention # (415) 781-0500.
Ideation, attempts, fears — past or current. For violence, note whether client was victim or perpetrator.
Direct but warm: "Have you ever had thoughts of not wanting to be here, or of hurting yourself?" · "Are you having any of those thoughts now?" · If yes: "Do you have a plan? Have you acted on these thoughts before?"
"Have you ever had thoughts of hurting someone else?" · Stay calm and matter-of-fact — asking directly gives them permission to be honest.
List medication, dosage; indicate if not prescribed or not being monitored.
"Are you taking any medications for your mental health right now?" · "Who prescribes those — a psychiatrist, your regular doctor?" · "How's that been working for you?"

Body Image / Disordered Eating

Regular, overeating, compulsive, binge/purge, fasting (when, how often). Eating disorder history? Prior treatment / hospitalizations?
Open and non-judgmental: "How's your relationship with food and eating these days?" · "Is your relationship with food or your body something that's ever felt difficult?" · Follow up only as far as they're comfortable going.

Medical History

Major illness, disabilities, hospitalizations, current medications (note date/age of significant occurrences).
"How's your physical health overall?" · "Any major illnesses, surgeries, or ongoing conditions I should know about?" · "Are you taking any medications for physical health?"

Substance Use / Abuse History

Normalize it: "I ask everyone about substances — caffeine and nicotine all the way through alcohol and other drugs. There's no judgment here, it just helps me get the full picture."
"Do you drink coffee or use nicotine at all?" · "How about alcohol — do you drink? Roughly how often, and how much?" · "Any other substances, recreational or otherwise?"
Explore the client's willingness to cut back on alcohol and other substances if use is more than occasional.

Family History

"What was it like in your family growing up? What did it look like? Feel like? What were your relationships with your family members like?"

Identity

"Is there anything about how you identify — culture, sexuality, gender, other parts of your background — that you'd like me to know about?"

Anything Else

Anything else the client feels is important to them and would like you to know.
"Is there anything we haven't touched on that feels important for me to know?" · "Anything you walked in here hoping you'd get a chance to say?"

Additional Questions (as time / relevance allows)

Follow the client's lead — these are doorways to open if it feels right, not a checklist to march through.

Other sources of stress (SES, food insufficiency, institutional racism, oppression, marginalization). How does the client cope? Support systems?
"What's life feeling like outside of what brought you in — work, money, housing, all of it?" · "When things get hard, what helps you get through?" · "Who do you have in your corner?"
Motivation, appetite, sleep disturbance (hours/night, insomnia, hypersomnia, disturbing dreams).
"How's your energy and motivation been lately?" · "How are you sleeping — and eating?" · "How would you describe your mood most days?"
Family history, messages/experiences growing up, substance use by family members.
"What did you learn about relationships growing up?" · "Were there messages in your family about how to be, or how to handle feelings?" · "Was substance use part of the picture at home?"
Abuse of client (emotional, physical, sexual, psychological) — if client wants to talk about it: when did it occur, begin, end, etc.?
Gentle, never pushed: "Have you ever experienced anything — emotional, physical, sexual — that felt harmful or unsafe?" · "We don't have to go into it today; I just want to know it's part of your story." · Let them set the pace.
Social/support systems, communication issues, current romantic/intimate relationship(s). Is current sexual life/expression satisfactory — if not, what would they like different? History of intimate relationships. Sexual expression if important to client (heterosexual, bisexual, gay/lesbian, queer, celibate, asexual, questioning). History / possible trauma around coming out, social support/community.
"Are you in a relationship right now? What's that like?" · "How connected do you feel to the people around you?" · "Is your intimate or sexual life somewhere you'd want anything to be different?" · Mirror the client's own language for their identity and relationships.
Transgender, gender non-conforming, intersex, genderqueer, two spirit, etc. History / possible trauma around coming out, social support/community.
"Is gender something that feels important to how you understand yourself?" · "How do you like to be referred to — name, pronouns?" · "Has finding support around that been easy or hard for you?"
